## Candidate Models

**Model A: Dirichlet-multinomial time-series model.**  
Let \(y_{t,k}\) be the count in outcome category \(k\), \(k=1,\ldots,7\) (guesses 1–6 and failure), and \(n_t=\sum_k y_{t,k}\). Use

\[
y_t\sim\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}(n_t,\alpha_t),\qquad
\log \alpha_{t,k}= \beta_k+x_t^\top\gamma_k+u_{t,k},
\]

with random walk or autoregressive \(u_{t,k}\). This preserves compositional dependence and supports uncertainty intervals.

**Model B: Ordered/hierarchical classification.**  
Model individual or aggregated outcomes using cumulative logits,

\[
\operatorname{logit}P(G_t\le j)=\theta_j-x_t^\top\beta,\quad j=1,\ldots,6,
\]

with optional word-level features (letter frequency, repeated letters, positional entropy) only if they are present or derivable from the permitted dataset. A multinomial logistic model is a simpler fallback if ordering is empirically inappropriate.

At most these two model families should be compared for each question; feature engineering and sensitivity analyses are not additional model families.

## Baseline

A persistence/seasonal baseline predicts the next day’s composition as the previous observed composition, with a rolling historical mean as a second baseline. For count data, use the prior-day empirical proportions and evaluate with weighted log loss and compositional distance. Baseline results are **pending execution**.

## Mathematical Specification

For prediction date \(t+h\), estimate parameters only on \(1{:}t\). Produce:

- prediction intervals from posterior simulation or block bootstrap;
- optional ranking of days/words by predicted failure probability.

Apply simplex normalization only after documenting whether the source percentages are rounded, incomplete, or exposure-weighted.
