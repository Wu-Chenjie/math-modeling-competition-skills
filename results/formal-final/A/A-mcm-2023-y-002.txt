## Problem Framing

Estimate and explain December 2020 advertised prices of used 36–56 ft sailboats across Europe, the Caribbean, and the USA. The response is listing price in USD; predictors are hull type, make, variant, length, geographic region, detailed location, and manufacture year. The model should distinguish depreciation, size, brand/variant, hull type, and location effects while producing defensible out-of-sample predictions.

Because the supplied excerpt ends mid-sentence, no additional task-specific deliverables beyond this supported scope are assumed.

Listing prices are not completed-sale prices, so the response measures seller expectations rather than realized market value.

## Assumptions

1. USD prices are comparable because all listings share the December 2020 snapshot.
2. Boat age is `2020 − manufacture year`; invalid or future years are excluded or corrected only with auditable rules.
3. Observations are listings, conditionally independent except for shared make/variant effects.
4. Unobserved condition, equipment, and seller urgency contribute irreducible error.
5. Monohulls and catamarans may have different price structures; pooling is allowed only with hull interactions.
6. Cleaning and imputation are learned inside each training fold to prevent leakage.

## Candidate Models

**Model 1: regularized hierarchical log-price regression.** Use nonlinear length and age terms, hull interactions, geographic fixed effects, and partially pooled make/variant effects. This is the primary interpretable model.

**Model 2: gradient-boosted trees.** Encode categorical variables without using target information from validation rows. This model captures nonlinearities and interactions and serves as a predictive challenger.

Rare categories should be pooled or handled hierarchically. Separate hull-type models will be compared with a pooled interaction model.

## Baseline

Predict the training-set median log price, first globally and then by hull type. A stronger baseline predicts the median within hull type × length band × age band, with fallback to broader groups. All medians are recomputed within each fold.

## Mathematical Specification

For listing \(i\),

\[
y_i=\log(1+P_i),
\]

\[
y_i=\beta_0+\beta_HH_i+f_L(L_i)+f_A(A_i)
+H_i f_{HL}(L_i)+H_i f_{HA}(A_i)
+\gamma_{R_i}+u_{M_i}+v_{V_i}+\epsilon_i.
\]

Here \(P_i\) is price, \(H_i\) hull type, \(L_i\) length, \(A_i\) age, \(R_i\) region, and \(u_M,v_V\) regularized make and variant effects. Splines or restricted polynomial bases define \(f_L\) and \(f_A\). Predictions return to dollars using an estimated retransformation correction derived only from training residuals.

Compare against both baselines with fold-level paired differences. Inspect residuals by hull type, length, age, region, make frequency, and price decile. ## Sensitivity and Robustness

Vary outlier rules, rare-category thresholds, spline complexity, regularization, and retransformation method. Compare pooled versus hull-specific models, random versus grouped folds, and inclusion versus exclusion of detailed location. Bootstrap prediction intervals and model rankings. Report degradation under leave-one-region-out tests.

## Falsification

Reject the preferred model if it fails to beat the hull-median baseline consistently, produces implausible age or length effects, collapses under grouped validation, shows severe regional residual bias, or depends materially on a few extreme listings. Permuted targets must eliminate predictive performance; unexpectedly strong performance would indicate leakage.

## Reviewer Risks

Principal risks are target leakage through category encoding, overfitting rare makes/variants, metric mismatch on a heavy-tailed price distribution, treating advertisements as sales, hidden duplicate listings, and unsupported causal interpretations.
