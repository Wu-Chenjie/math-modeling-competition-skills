## Problem Framing

**Run:** A-mcm-2023-y-001  
**Case:** 2023 MCM Problem Y, *Understanding Used Sailboat Prices*

Develop a reproducible model of December 2020 advertised sailboat prices using hull type, make, variant, length, geographic region, detailed location, and manufacture year. The analysis should:

1. Predict listing price for comparable boats.
2. Quantify how age, size, hull type, brand/model, and geography relate to price.
3. Report predictive uncertainty and identify observations outside the model’s reliable domain.

The supplied excerpt is truncated before the complete requirements. Therefore, any undisclosed subquestions are out of scope. Listing prices must not be interpreted as transaction values or causal valuations.

## Assumptions

- All listings represent the stated December 2020 market snapshot.
- Prices are already comparable nominal USD amounts.
- Manufacture year does not exceed 2020; define age as \(2020-\text{year}\).
- Each row is provisionally treated as a distinct listing, subject to duplicate testing.
- Missingness is not assumed random until patterns are examined.
- Predictions apply only to 36–56 ft used sailboats and represented markets.
- Hull-type effects may interact with age, length, and geography.

## Candidate Models

**Model A: regularized hedonic regression.** Fit log price using nonlinear age and length terms, hull type, geography, make/variant effects, and selected interactions. Elastic-net regularization controls sparse categories and provides an interpretable benchmark.

**Model B: gradient-boosted decision trees.** Use the same admissible predictors, fold-safe categorical processing, missing-value handling, and constrained tuning. This captures nonlinearities and interactions without prespecifying their exact form.

Models will be compared on identical held-out folds. Complexity is accepted only when it improves out-of-sample performance and calibration.

## Baseline

Use the training-fold median log price within hull type, falling back to the global training median when necessary. This establishes whether feature-based models improve meaningfully over simple market segmentation.

## Math Specification

For listing \(i\),

\[
y_i=\log(1+P_i),\qquad
A_i=2020-Y_i.
\]

Model A is

\[
y_i=\beta_0+f_A(A_i)+f_L(\log L_i)+
\beta_HH_i+\gamma_{R_i}+u_{\text{make}(i)}
+v_{\text{variant}(i)}
+\delta^\top z_i+\epsilon_i,
\]

where \(f_A,f_L\) are spline or polynomial terms and \(z_i\) contains preregistered hull-by-age, hull-by-length, and hull-by-region interactions. Estimate coefficients by minimizing

\[
\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2+
\lambda\left[\alpha\|\theta\|_1+(1-\alpha)\|\theta\|_2^2\right].
\]

Back-transform with a fold-estimated smearing factor. Report RMSLE, log-scale MAE, dollar MAE, and median absolute percentage error. Avoid relying on MAPE alone.

## Sensitivity and Robustness

Repeat evaluation under alternative duplicate rules, missing-value treatments, rare-category thresholds, pooled versus hull-specific models, and train-only price trimming. Bootstrap by make–variant groups. Examine performance after excluding the highest-price tail and under leave-one-region-out tests.

## Falsification

- Shuffled-target models should collapse to baseline performance.
- Deliberately leaked target encodings should reveal an implausible validation jump.
- Predictions should not systematically decrease with length among otherwise comparable boats without supporting evidence.
- Residual location or make patterns would falsify adequate adjustment.
- Poor unseen-region or unseen-variant performance would falsify broad generalizability.
