## 3. Assumptions

Pending verification:

- Analysis units are location–time observations.
- A measurable quantity such as nighttime radiance can serve as an exposure proxy, but not automatically as total ecological or health harm.
- Relevant predictors may include land use, population or infrastructure intensity, lighting characteristics, weather, and natural background conditions.
- Nearby observations are spatially dependent.
- Observational associations may be confounded and must not be presented as intervention effects.
- If several impacts are combined, weights must be reported and varied rather than treated as objectively correct.

## 4. Candidate models

**Model A: Spatial hierarchical regression.** Model transformed exposure using nonlinear covariate effects plus a spatial random field. This directly addresses spatial dependence, supports uncertainty intervals, and permits interpretable scenario changes.

**Model B: Physics-informed propagation model.** Represent emitted light from multiple sources with distance attenuation, atmospheric transmission, shielding, and background illumination. This is preferable when source-level engineering data exist and provides more defensible intervention mechanisms.

Model selection will depend on available resolution and variables. The two approaches may be compared, but their outputs must not be blended without calibration.

## 5. Baseline

Use an intercept-only predictor and a non-spatial regularized linear regression. Both must be evaluated on identical held-out spatial blocks. A complex model is justified only if it improves predictive error, calibration, and residual spatial structure.

## 6. Mathematical specification

For observation \(i\), let \(y_i=\log(1+L_i)\), where \(L_i\) is measured exposure; \(x_i\) are covariates; and \(s_i\) is location:

\[
y_i=\beta_0+\sum_j f_j(x_{ij})+u(s_i)+\epsilon_i,
\qquad
\epsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2),
\]

\[
\operatorname{Cov}[u(s_i),u(s_k)]
=\tau^2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|s_i-s_k\|}{\rho}\right).
\]

For scenario \(a\), alter only specified actionable covariates:

\[
\Delta_i^{(a)}
=\widehat L_i(x_i^{(a)},s_i)-\widehat L_i(x_i,s_i).
\]

Report distributions of \(\Delta_i^{(a)}\), not unsupported point claims. If impacts \(R_{ik}\) are aggregated,

\[
R_i(w)=\sum_k w_kR_{ik},\quad w_k\ge0,\quad \sum_k w_k=1,
\]

with conclusions evaluated over a preregistered weight simplex.

## 8. Validation

Primary metrics: MAE and RMSE on the original exposure scale. Also report interval coverage, calibration, and residual spatial autocorrelation. Compare performance by geographic or settlement strata where sample sizes permit. No random-row cross-validation should be used as the sole validation because it can leak nearby spatial information.

## 9. Sensitivity and robustness

Vary spatial block size, covariance kernel, transformation, influential observations, missing-data treatment, exposure definition, scenario intensity, and aggregation weights. Repeat analysis after geographic reweighting to assess sampling bias. Report rank or recommendation stability rather than one preferred configuration alone.

## 10. Falsification

Reject or qualify the framework if:

- spatially permuted outcomes perform similarly;
- held-out-region error greatly exceeds internal validation error;
- residual spatial dependence remains substantial;
- estimated effects reverse across plausible specifications;
- negative-control predictors appear strongly predictive;
- placebo interventions produce material changes;
- uncertainty intervals fail empirical coverage.
