## Problem Framing

Maasai Mara is treated as a coupled conservation and community-resource system. The decision problem is to recommend management policies for resources inside and outside the current preserve boundary while balancing:

- biodiversity and habitat connectivity;
- local-community benefits and equitable resource sharing;
- tourism/revenue generation;
- enforcement and implementation cost;
- ecological and operational capacity.

The supplied excerpt identifies policy recommendation and boundary-aware management as the core requirement. Exact downstream sub-question wording should be checked against the official statement before implementation.

## Assumptions

Provisional assumptions requiring confirmation:

1. Management units can be represented as nodes (habitat blocks, community areas, tourism zones, or corridors).
2. Policies have measurable effects on ecological, social, financial, and enforcement outcomes.
3. Resource, budget, visitor, and enforcement capacities are finite.
4. Benefits and costs can be normalized before aggregation.
5. Uncertainty is represented through scenarios rather than hidden point estimates.
6. Community outcomes require explicit equity constraints, not only an aggregate welfare objective.

## Mathematical Specification

Let \(G=(V,E(y))\) be the management network, where \(y_{ij}\in\{0,1\}\) indicates a corridor or cooperation link. Let \(x_i\in[0,1]\) denote intensity of policy \(i\).

For scenario \(s\), compute:

\[
F_s(x,y)=\big(B_s,\;W_s,\;R_s,\;-C_s\big),
\]

where \(B\) is biodiversity/connectivity, \(W\) community welfare/equity, \(R\) revenue, and \(C\) implementation/enforcement cost.

A Pareto portfolio may be found by:

\[
\max_{x,y}\; \sum_s p_s
\left[
w_B\tilde B_s+w_W\tilde W_s+w_R\tilde R_s-w_C\tilde C_s
\right]
-\rho\,\mathrm{SD}_s(U_s)
\]

subject to:

\[
\sum_i c_i x_i\le C_{\max},\qquad
\sum_{i\in g} b_i x_i\ge Q_g^{\min},
\]

\[
v_{ij}(x)\le K_{ij},\qquad
\mathrm{Conn}(G)\ge L_{\min},\qquad
x_i\in[0,1].
\]

Here \(C_{\max}\) is budget capacity, \(Q_g^{\min}\) is a community-equity floor, \(K_{ij}\) are ecological or visitor capacities, and \(\mathrm{Conn}(G)\) is a connectivity measure such as reachable habitat coverage or algebraic connectivity. Weights should be elicited or sensitivity-tested, never chosen arbitrarily.

## Falsification Criteria

Reject a recommendation if it violates any hard capacity or equity constraint, disconnects required habitat corridors, is dominated by the status quo, depends on a single arbitrary weight vector, or loses its advantage across plausible scenarios. Any causal claim unsupported by supplied evidence must be labeled as an assumption.

## Reviewer Risks

Primary risks are exactly those flagged in the benchmark metadata:

- arbitrary objective weights;
- ignored resource, visitor, or enforcement capacity;
- no robustness analysis.

Additional risks include fabricated empirical parameters, double-counting tourism and community benefits, treating graph links as guaranteed causation, and reporting unsupported numerical performance.
