## Candidate Models

**Model 1: Network analysis.** Construct a multilayer graph with nodes for park zones, surrounding communities, tourism operators, government/enforcement bodies, and wildlife corridors. Edge weights represent movement, revenue, service provision, or conflict exposure. Centrality, connectivity, and bottleneck measures identify critical corridors and intervention points.

**Model 2: Multi-objective optimization.** Select a policy portfolio maximizing ecological benefit, community benefit, and net financial value while minimizing conflict and enforcement risk. Use Pareto optimization or epsilon-constraint generation to expose trade-offs. Scenario analysis evaluates robustness under tourism, funding, climate, and enforcement conditions.

These are the two independent model systems; scenario analysis is a stress-testing layer, not a third model.

## Math Specification

Let \(x_j\in\{0,1\}\) indicate adoption of policy \(j\), and \(y_{iz}\in[0,1]\) indicate intensity of policy \(j\) in zone \(i\). Let \(s\) index scenarios.

For each scenario, maximize:

\[
F_1(x)=\text{ecological protection},\quad
F_2(x)=\text{community benefit},\quad
F_3(x)=\text{financial net benefit},
\]

while minimizing conflict/enforcement risk \(F_4(x)\).

A scalar reporting form is:

\[
\max_x\; \sum_{k=1}^{4} w_k \,\tilde F_k(x),
\]

with \(\tilde F_k\) normalized using documented bounds and weights \(w_k\) elicited or stress-tested, never chosen arbitrarily.

Subject to:

\[
\sum_j c_jx_j\le C,\qquad
\sum_j r_{ij}x_j\le R_i,\qquad
x_j\in\{0,1\},
\]

plus ecological minimums, corridor-connectivity requirements, equity constraints, and policy incompatibilities identified from the full problem statement. Robust selection can maximize worst-case or lower-quantile scenario performance.
