## Problem Framing

Let \(x_{ij}\) be the reported percentage of component \(j\) for observation \(i\), \(s_i=\sum_jx_{ij}\), \(T_i\) the known type, \(W_i\) weathering status, and \(A_i\) artifact ID.

The central complication is compositional data: components are constrained by a total, and weathering changes observed proportions. Repeated rows for one artifact must be grouped during validation to avoid leakage.

Required outputs:

1. Estimate associations of weathering with type, pattern, and color; quantify composition shifts and reconstruct plausible pre-weathering composition.
2. Classify high-potassium versus lead-barium glass, then identify stable within-type subtypes.
3. Assign each unknown sample a type with uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.
4. Compare component-association structures between types.

## Three Candidate Model Families

### 1. Interpretable compositional regression

Use zero/non-detection handling followed by closure and an isometric log-ratio transform (ILR). Fit:

- grouped logistic or mixed-effects regression for \(W_i\);
- multivariate linear/mixed regression for transformed composition;
- within-type Gaussian mixtures for subtypes.

Strength: transparent coefficients, confounder controls, and reconstructable compositions. Risk: sensitivity to zero replacement and distributional assumptions.

### 2. Nonlinear supervised classification

Use ILR balances as inputs to calibrated SVM, random forest, or gradient boosting models.

Strength: captures nonlinear boundaries and interactions. Risk: small effective sample size, repeated-artifact leakage, and reduced interpretability. Must be compared against the baseline, not reported alone.

### 3. Probabilistic latent compositional model

Use a logistic-normal hierarchical model with artifact/location random effects, measurement-error sensitivity, and latent subtype mixtures. Estimate type and subtype posteriors jointly.

Strength: uncertainty propagation and explicit treatment of repeated measurements. Risk: computational complexity, identifiability, and dependence on prior/model specification.

## Baseline Contract

A simple, reproducible baseline should complete the same outputs:

- Type: nearest-centroid or linear discriminant classifier in ILR space; alternatively a one-balance rule based on learned \( \tau \) for \(PbO+BaO\) versus \(K_2O\). The value of \( \tau \) is **pending estimation**.
- Subtype: within-type \(k\)-means or Gaussian mixture, with \(k\) selected only from training data.
- Unknowns: posterior/nearest-centroid assignment plus a low-confidence reject option.
- Weathering: group-wise mean composition difference between weathered and unweathered observations.
- Associations: pairwise CLR/ILR correlation matrix by type.

No baseline result has been executed.

## Mathematical Specification

1. **Validity and normalization**

\[
s_i=\sum_{j=1}^{p}x_{ij},\qquad I_i=\mathbf 1(85\le s_i\le105),\qquad
y_{ij}=x_{ij}/s_i.
\]

Rows with \(I_i=0\) are excluded or handled in a pre-registered sensitivity branch.

2. **Compositional transform**

After a declared non-detection replacement rule,

\[
z_i=V^\top\log(y_i),
\]

where \(V\) is an orthonormal ILR contrast matrix.

3. **Weathering model**

\[
\operatorname{logit}P(W_i=1)
=\alpha+\beta_TT_i+\beta_PP_i+\beta_CC_i+u_{A_i}.
\]

For transformed composition:

\[
z_i=\mu+\gamma_TT_i+\gamma_WW_i+
\gamma_{TW}(T_iW_i)+\gamma_PP_i+\gamma_CC_i+u_{A_i}+\epsilon_i.
\]

A proposed pre-weathering estimate is

\[
\widehat z_{\text{pre}}=\widehat z_{\text{weathered}}
-\widehat\gamma_W-\widehat\gamma_{TW}T,
\]

followed by inverse ILR and closure. This reconstruction is **pending execution and validation**.

4. **Type and subtype**

\[
P(T_i=\text{Pb-Ba}\mid z_i)=
\sigma(a+b^\top z_i),
\]

and, within type \(t\),

\[
p(z_i\mid T_i=t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_t}
\pi_{tk}\mathcal N(z_i;\mu_{tk},\Sigma_{tk}).
\]

5. **Association comparison**

For each type, estimate a sparse precision matrix:

\[
\widehat\Theta_t=
\arg\max_{\Theta\succ0}
\{\log\det\Theta-\operatorname{tr}(S_t\Theta)
-\lambda\|\Theta\|_1\}.
\]

Compare edge presence, sign, and bootstrap stability across types.

## Validation Plan

- Split by artifact ID, never by chemistry row; location-specific rows must remain in one fold.
- Use grouped nested cross-validation for type/subtype models.
- Report balanced accuracy, macro-F1, log loss/Brier score, confusion matrices, and bootstrap intervals.
- Compare invalid-row handling: exclude, renormalize, and bounded/imputed sensitivity branches.
- Compare multiple non-detection replacements and perturbations.
- Validate pre-weathering reconstruction against held-out known unweathered measurements and paired locations where available.
- For unknowns, report posterior confidence and perturbation stability; true labels are unavailable in the attachment.
- For association graphs, use bootstrap edge-selection frequency and class-label permutation tests.
- Record seed, fold assignments, parameters, runtime, input/output hashes, and exact command.

## Falsification Questions

- Does the weathering effect remain after controlling for type, pattern, color, and artifact?
- Does row-wise splitting produce inflated performance compared with artifact-grouped splitting?
- Do conclusions change under alternative blank/non-detection treatment?
- Do the 85%-105% validity boundaries materially change results?
- Is classification driven only by \(PbO\), \(BaO\), and \(K_2O\)?
- Are subtype counts stable across seeds, folds, and model families?
- Does pre-weathering correction recover known unweathered compositions?
- Do association-network differences survive bootstrap and label permutation?
- Are unknown classifications stable under measurement perturbations?
